  1. 5 B 70 hand comparison 2026
    The purchase note of 30 November 1560 and the marginalia of NK ČR 5 B 70 compared, on the Manuscriptorium facsimile, with the two ‘Iohannes Pratensis’ signatures on the same title page, with Tycho's dated specimens of 1583 (KB GKS 1823) and 1599 (the album leaf of 8 May), and with the two hands of the volume of 1561 at Helsingør, on 25 October 2026: the scribe-compare method (each specimen's features extracted blind, the vectors compared, every claim audited against the images), with the pipeline's three verdict bands. Every pair returned ‘inconclusive’: the note against the signatures with a weak lean against identity (the h built on different limbs, pointed against rounded minims; the shared capital I the period's standard form), against Tycho's specimens with a weak lean against (the capital I without the J-descender, the final s at x-height against his descending final s), against the hands of 1561 with a weak lean against; the marginalia against Tycho's specimens with a weak-to-moderate lean against (the u-marks and the looped g agree; the open e, the 3-shaped final s, the absence of the ſt/ct arch and the j-form final i do not — a German-cursive repertoire the secure hands show nowhere). Sample lengths below the forensic floor; the facsimile's c. 15–20 px x-height for the note is the binding constraint. Unpublished; the crops, the claims and the audits are kept with the edition's working papers.
    Contributes The hands of the note and the marginalia tested against Pratensis and against Tycho: not shown to be Tycho's, not shown to be Pratensis's; the marginalia leaning away from Tycho.

  7. Bargum 1906–07
    G. Bargum, ‘Über Tycho Brahes Buchdruck und Bucheinbände’, Zeitschrift für Bücherfreunde 10 (1906/07), Beiblatt (Chronik), pp. 124–126, signed ‘Kopenhagen. G. Bargum’. A reply to the binding note of Heft 1 and to Kukula: the Uraniborg press was at work by 1584, not 1596; the plaques served as the mark of Tycho's library and as his printer's and publisher's device; Hasselberg's three binding types; the German library copies of the Mechanica; the Zwinger volume ‘kein schweizer, sondern ebenfalls ein dänischer, unter Tychos persönlicher Anleitung hergestellter’ binding, though the fourteen medallions have no parallel on his other bindings — Tycho was not at Basel in 1587, and Gellius Sascerides, who set out in the summer of 1588, may have procured the book at Basel or at the Frankfurt fair; Kukula's conjecture of late luxury bindings answered with the Ramus and Maestlin–Clavius volumes; and the suggestion that Tycho took from Zwinger's title the name of his projected Theatrum astronomicum.
    Contributes The first argument that the Röhsska binding is Danish work, and the Theatrum astronomicum remark.
