Tycho’s books are recognised by the marks his binders and his own hand put on them. The bindings form a datable sequence. In the student years the name was stamped on the boards — TVCHO BRADE / ANNO 1561 on the volume now at Helsingør, TY BRAD 1563 on the Uppsala Sammelband, TYCHO / BRAHE 1567 on the Strahov Euclid — or the initials alone with the year, T. B. 1560 (18 J 318) and 1567 (10 G 47); all of these are earlier than Hven. From 1576, when Uraniborg was furnished, the gilt cipher T.B.O. (Tychonis Brahe Ottonidis — Prusík 1901, p. 198) stands on brown leather with the year beneath it, 1576 on most of the books, then 1580, 1582 (Kleinschnitzová 1933, p. 81) and 1583 (Gingerich 2002); where these bindings were made is not settled — Prandtl places a binder on Hven (1932, p. 9), Kleinschnitzová argues a Copenhagen workshop from the stamps shared with Vedel’s bindings (1933, p. 82); she also recorded Danish waste in two boards — a Danish print, an arithmetic she supposed, with a Dutch gospel print in the T.B.O. 1576 binding of 14 A 66 (no. 34), and a Danish manuscript fragment of the seventeenth century in the rebinding of 40 F 39 (no. 14). From 1588 the presentation bindings carry the gilt portrait-and-arms supralibros, Prandtl’s Prachtband (1932, p. 11); one of them, the Strahov Opus palatinum, was struck after 1599 by a Prague binder of the Rudolfine circle (Voit 2020). Books received ready-bound, and gifts, may carry only his handwritten initials or a purchase-note. The four periods are Prandtl’s (Verzeichnis B, 1932, pp. 13–14), after Prusík (1901, p. 198 n.); the date ranges are this catalogue’s, from the dated stamps, and are the scale used in the volume records.

The dies of period 4 are dated by the presentation copies of De mundi (1588): the Sascerides copy, Royal Danish Library LN 428 4° ex. 1, its dedication dated 20 March 1588 (Norlind 1970, p. 122; the census of De mundi); the Scultetus copy at Görlitz, portrait and arms, dedication dated 8 May 1588 (Norlind 1970, p. 124); the Maestlin copy in the British Library, dedication dated 14 May 1588 (Norlind 1970, p. 122).

36 of the 72 volumes carry no binding period in their record: books in another owner’s binding or rebound after they left the library, manuscripts, bindings stripped of their covering, and bindings not described at first hand.

Dated T.B.O. stamps recorded on the volumes catalogued here (volumes, by the year stamped): 1576 (13); 1580 (1); 1582 (1); 1583 (1).

Recognition checklist

  • An oval gilt portrait medallion on the upper board, with the distich ‘Hic patet exterior Tychonis forma Brahei…’, in two sizes — about 76 × 58 and 58 × 43 mm, as measured in the table below — indicates a presentation binding of period 4, Prandtl’s Prachtband (1932, p. 11).
  • The arms oval on the lower board, with ‘Arma genus, fundi pereunt; durabile virtus…’, accompanies the portrait.
  • The gilt cipher T.B.O. on brown calf with a rhomboid arabesque and the year beneath indicates period 3; the same cipher with no year reported is Prandtl’s period 2.
  • Handwritten ‘T. B.’ initials or a purchase-note on the title-page mark gifts and copies received ready-bound.
  • The Jesuit accession inscription ‘A. 1642 / Ex Bibliotheca Tychoniana’, or a Chomutov, Krumlov or professed-house catalogue entry (Chomutov 1645–1675, two of them Crusius’s gifts of 1654), indicates the Jesuit road — the Clementinum, or a Jesuit house whose library came to it after 1773.

A caution. The Clementinum’s inscription Ex Bibliotheca Tychoniana is not by itself proof of Tycho’s ownership: the Jesuits went on adding to the Tychoniana after his death. Sign. 14 J 199, two Kepler tracts of 1606 bound together, carries the inscription. Kukula (1906–07, p. 25) and Prandtl (1932, p. 16) read it as a sign that the heirs and Kepler kept the library together and added to it; Kleinschnitzová excluded it from her census because Tycho died in 1601 (1933, p. 83). It is recorded here among the volumes marked Tychoniana but not his. Conversely, a volume bearing only the Jesuit inscription and no binding-stamp may still be Tycho’s where its imprint predates 1601: on that ground Kleinschnitzová admitted the plain-bound Clementinum volumes (1933, p. 83).
