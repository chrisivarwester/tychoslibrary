Acquisition
1561; bought as a student with the Regiomontanus in the same volume: the purchase note ‘Eptg [emptus] thalero & 4 Groshj’ stands on the volume’s fly-title and the binding is stamped ‘Anno 1561’ (Prandtl 1932, Band II; Klem, Årbog 1957, p. 8) (Direct purchase)
Ownership marks
Inscription: the ownership inscriptions and the purchase note stand on the fly-title of the Regiomontanus, first in the volume Annotations: fol. 53r–v: four places with their positions in an early hand, read by the museum as ‘Elzingborgum 35 46. 57 0 / Lunda Lundt 36 30 57 23 / Vesby Gotlandiæ 47 5. 59 50 / Rumefalche [?] 46 35 60 15’. The Helsingborg and Lund figures are the book's own printed values from the Scania table on fol. 41v (‘Elseburgum 35 46 | 57 0’, ‘Lundis vel Lunda 36 30 | 57 23’), transcribed beside the printed Zealand–Scania island entry; Visby and the fourth place, which the printed table lacks, stand beside the Gotland entry on fol. 53v, so the fourth place was taken for a Gotland or neighbouring-island locality; it is Schöner's ‘Rumefalche’ (Luculentissima quaedam terrae totius descriptio, 1515, fo. 34r: ‘Bespue 47.5.59.50. Rumefalche 46.35.60.15.’), a Clavus-derived pseudo-toponym of Gotland from the Ulm Ptolemy of 1486 (Bjørnbo and Petersen 1904, pp. 115, 118), identified 21 October 2026. The latitudes are the Ptolemaic values Tycho later corrected (Helsingborg 56 3, Lund 55 45: TBOO V, 309–312), so the entries predate his Danish latitude programme; the hand is undated and uncompared. Photographed 20 October 2026: the four lines are in one ink and ductus — ‘Elzingborgum’ with a superscript c above the name, ‘Lunda’, ‘Vesbÿ Gotlandiæ’, and a fourth whose letters read R-?-n-s-f-a-l-?-h-?; read again on 21 October 2026 as ‘Lůnda lůndt’, ‘Veſbÿ Gotlandiæ’ and ‘Rumefalche’ (the museum's reading stands; the tall letter is an f), the u-marks and the diaeresis being the writer's habit; Visby and Rumefalche are Schöner's two Gotland ‘cities’ with his figures, Helsingborg and Lund his figures as Apian reprinted them. Also by hand: ‘Octava sphaera’ at the ring of the Schema praemissae divisionis, and ‘Wittenberga’ underlined in the German place table Binding: Band II: brown calf, blind-ruled mirror panels on both boards, originally gilt floral stamps, TVCHO BRADE stamped on the front board, ANNO 1561 on the rear, three raised bands; on the spine the remnant of a label with a library shelfmark 204 (the museum's record, 1957:0030)

Surviving copy
1957:0030, M/S Museet for Søfart (Maritime Museum of Denmark) · identification A · Period 1 (student bindings, 1560–1567)
front board: TVCHO BRADE, rear board: ANNO 1561 (stamped, originally gilt); fly-title: ‘tico brade ven:’ (struck through), ‘Tycho Brade’ and further ownership inscriptions (leaf half torn, repaired); fly-title verso: ‘Sum Thÿchonis Brade’ [I am Tycho Brahe's] (struck through), ‘Eptg [emptus] thalero & 4 Groshj’ [bought for a thaler and 4 groschen], beneath it an astronomical note, partly lost; a note in ink on the front pastedown; fol. 53r–v of the Apianus: four places with their positions in Tycho's hand; on the spine the remnant of a label with a library shelfmark 204; Latin plant names at the head of a run of recto pages in a hand other than Tycho's, and small plant remains between leaves (Magasin 1987, p. 31; Björklund 1992, p. 26; confirmed 20 October 2026); at the head of the front pastedown the pencil figure ‘+125’, Maggs's price of 1933, and a second figure (the museum's photograph). Thykier (1985 inspection; report of 13 January 2003) reads the front of the fly-title (his ‘forperm’) as the autograph ‘Tyge Brahe’ struck through and ‘tico brahe ven’ struck through (‘en lidt barnlig skrift’ [a somewhat childish hand]), and in another hand ‘Sum Paul…’, the rest lost on the torn-off part; on its back, under a struck line where ‘Brade’ can be glimpsed, the price ‘… 4 taliro & 4 Gross.’; short astronomical notes in Tycho's hand inside the binding and on the back of the leaf, and one in the margin of the preface's tenth (unpaginated) page; Latin plant names at the head of 36 unpaginated pages in a hand other than Tycho's, plant remains in the gutter of 26 of them (microscoped by Jette Baagøe, re-housed in annotated envelopes of acid-free silk paper), further remains between pages without names, one plant name unattested before c. 1623 (Peter Wagner and Baagøe). Examined and photographed at the museum on 20 October 2026. Fly-title recto: ‘tico brade ven:’ in a thin early hand, struck through once in the same ink; beside it a name obliterated under heavy black strokes (Thykier: ‘Tyge Brahe’), ringed in pencil; to the right, in a firmer hand with a flourish, ‘Sum Pauli’ [I am Paul's], complete to its last letter, what followed lost with the torn-away part; above it a smudged short word in another ink, and at the head, right, a short word in a darker ink, both unread; at the head, left, pencil ‘Acc. 1986/116’. Fly-title verso: ‘Sum Thÿchonis Brade’ under a heavy bar, ‘Sum’ and ‘Brade’ legible beside it; below it the purchase note ‘Eptg thaliro & 4 Grossj’ [bought for a thaler and 4 groschen]; beneath that, intact, a two-branch memorandum in bracket form — ‘Decl: Merid: … obliqua {Adde / Subtr.} diff. asc. ab Ascensione Recta — Septen: … obliqua {Subtr. / Adde} diff. Asc. ab Asc. R.’, ‘obliqua’ struck and ‘Recta’ written above it — a rule for passing from right to oblique ascension by the sign of the declination. Front pastedown: an ink note in the same early hand (‘Ascens: decl: Merid: … ex Asc: Recta / Septm: Sub S … hoc modo’, a companion rule); a pencil collation in German (‘… 70 (von 1–74) … 31–34 … 1 Doppelbl. …’, the trace of the Munich trade); pencil ‘1986/116’. Spine: a paper slip in the second compartment with ‘20’ and a damaged third figure consistent with 4, the figures written by hand in ink; nothing else lettered, no label surviving at the tail. Rear flyleaf: a faint ‘N 36’ at the head and a small blind-stamped cartouche near the gutter, both unread. Rear pastedown: in ballpoint ‘Gave jan. 1957 fra civiling. Oskar Ekman’ [gift, January 1957, from the engineer Oskar Ekman], the stamp HANDELS- OG SØFARTSMUSEET PÅ KRONBORG HELSINGØR – DANMARK [the Trade and Maritime Museum at Kronborg, Helsingør, Denmark], and ‘30:57’ in blue. Box: brown cloth clamshell, its gilt spine label lettered in English — JOHANN DE MONTEREGIO TABULAE DIRECTIONUM 1550 BOUND WITH PETRUS APPIANUS COSMOGRAPHIA 1553. TYCHO BRAHE'S COPY WITH AUTOGRAPH OWNERSHIP NOTES, ETC. — with ‘30:57’ written on it. Reader's marks in the Regiomontanus: a grey marginal note in an early hand on the Octavum problema page (‘pro g. m. / 60 / sec. / 15 / tert. / differentia’ — Thykier's note on the tenth page of the preface); tallies ij iij iiij beside ‘Ille autem numerus polaris’; a red-brown stepped line drawn through the Tabula declinationum; a large pen flourish at the head of the Tabula ascensionum rectarum. In the Apianus: ‘Octava sphaera’ written at the ring of the Schema praemissae divisionis, ‘Wittenberga’ underlined in the German place table; on fol. 53r ‘Elzingborgum 35 46. 57 0’ (a superscript c above the name) and ‘Lunda 36 30 57 23’, on fol. 53v ‘Vesbÿ Gotlandiæ 47 5. 59 50’ and the fourth place, its letters R-?-n-s-f-a-l-?-h-?, ‘46 35 60 15’, all four in one ink and ductus. The plant names stand at the head of the preface leaves (Pulmonaria, Ranunculus aquaticus, Hedera arborescens …) in a later hand, and the envelopes of plant remains lie at their openings; read letter by letter from the frames on 21 October 2026: the purchase note is ‘Eptꝰ thaliro & 4 Groſs.’ — emptus thalero et 4 grossis — the sign after ‘Ept’ being the -us abbreviation, not a g; the pastedown rule reads in full ‘Aſcens. decl {Merid. [Adde, blotted out by the writer] / Septen. Sub} ex Aſc. Recta / in deſcenſionibꝫ contrario modo’, the same rule as the verso's bracket memorandum, whose surviving case labels are ‘…rom:’ and ‘…nes’; above the rule a darker ‘Angl. 5.’, unexplained; the smudged word above ‘Sum Paüli’ reads ‘Brÿſſe’ or ‘Chrÿſſe’ and stands inside a wet-wiped erasure of an earlier inscription, the head-right word ‘to bra[h/n][e/o]’; the heavy black strokes through the recto name and through the verso's ‘Sum Thÿchonis Brade’ are one ink and one campaign, a later owner's cancellation of both Brahe names, while the thin stroke through ‘tico brade ven:’ is earlier and different; the herbarium hand also wrote ‘Lamiꝫ’, ‘Liſimachia flore luteo’ and ‘Hÿacinthꝰ’; on fol. 53 ‘Lůnda lůndt’ and ‘Veſbÿ Gotlandiæ’ carry the u-mark and the diaeresis, and the fourth place is the museum's ‘Rumefalche’ (the tall letter is an f with a cross-bar), identified on 21 October 2026 as Schöner's — Luculentissima quaedam terrae totius descriptio (Nuremberg 1515), fo. 34r, ‘Gotlandiae insu. civita.: Bespue 47.5.59.50. Rumefalche 46.35.60.15.’ — a Clavus-derived pseudo-toponym of Gotland from the Ulm Ptolemy of 1486, so that both added lines on fol. 53v are copied, name and figures, from Schöner or from his source; a pencil ‘choo’ on the rear pastedown

Note
Prandtl Verzeichnis A no. 3; bound in Band II. The volume is at M/S Museet for Søfart, Helsingør, inventory 1957:0030, the gift of Lia and Oscar Ekman in 1957. The museum’s record gives the Paris 1553 title with the colophon dated 1551, fol. 1–74, the cordiform map inserted between fol. 34 and 35, and the four added positions on fol. 53r–v. The volume is in the museum’s own keeping; the Royal Library deposit reported in 1987 and 1992 has ended (the museum’s confirmation of 13 October 2026). Gingerich 2002 (p. 325) still recorded ‘a copy of the 1561 edition of Petrus Apianus’ Cosmographia’ as sold by a Munich antiquariat to an undisclosed location: this is the present volume, whose binding is stamped 1561. Examined at the museum on 20 October 2026: the four places read on the leaf, the herbarium confirmed, the accession note read. The fol. 53 lines read letter by letter on 21 October 2026; the hand compared with the Graphonomicon's dated specimens (inconclusive). The fourth place identified on 21 October 2026 as Schöner's Rumefalche (1515, fo. 34r), Clavus's pseudo-toponym; the misreading of its fifth letter in the transcription withdrawn. Reviewed on 29 October 2026: of the six volumes the house has carried as the student’s, this is the one documented as Tycho’s before he left Denmark on 14 February 1562 — by the boards stamped with his name and the year 1561, and by nothing else: the purchase note and the ownership entries are undated, and no hand in the volume is attributed. Copenhagen as the place of purchase is an inference from the year (the Kronik of 1987 read the Leipzig register’s ‘16 October 1561’ as the matriculation; it is the winter-semester heading, Norlind 1970, p. 16, and Tycho and Vedel matriculated in the spring of 1562); the price in thaler and groschen is German money of account, which no source explains for a Copenhagen purchase; Klem’s ‘i 1561’ (Årbog 1957, p. 8) is the binding’s year, not the note’s. The volume came back to Denmark as a gift from Lia Ekman and her late husband Oscar (the museum’s report of 1957), entered in January 1957 by the widow in the dead man’s name (he died 16 September 1956; the sale catalogue of 1965, lot 497: ‘according to the last will of ingeniør Ekman’, not for sale).
