Earlier catalogues
Prandtl, Verz. B, Band II
Identification
A · identified by Prandtl, Die Bibliothek des Tycho Brahe (Verzeichnis B); the museum's record and photographs (the binding stamps, the inscriptions leaf by leaf, the purchase note, the added positions on fol. 53 of the Apianus); examined and photographed at the museum on 20 October 2026 (the stamps TVCHO BRADE and ANNO 1561, the fly-title's inscriptions and purchase note on both sides of the leaf, the fol. 53 entries, the museum's accession note)
Marks securing the identification

front board: TVCHO BRADE, rear board: ANNO 1561 (stamped, originally gilt); fly-title: ‘tico brade ven:’ (struck through), ‘Tycho Brade’ and further ownership inscriptions (leaf half torn, repaired); fly-title verso: ‘Sum Thÿchonis Brade’ [I am Tycho Brahe's] (struck through), ‘Eptg [emptus] thalero & 4 Groshj’ [bought for a thaler and 4 groschen], beneath it an astronomical note, partly lost; a note in ink on the front pastedown; fol. 53r–v of the Apianus: four places with their positions in Tycho's hand; on the spine the remnant of a label with a library shelfmark 204; Latin plant names at the head of a run of recto pages in a hand other than Tycho's, and small plant remains between leaves (Magasin 1987, p. 31; Björklund 1992, p. 26; confirmed 20 October 2026); at the head of the front pastedown the pencil figure ‘+125’, Maggs's price of 1933, and a second figure (the museum's photograph).

Thykier (1985 inspection; report of 13 January 2003) reads the front of the fly-title (his ‘forperm’) as the autograph ‘Tyge Brahe’ struck through and ‘tico brahe ven’ struck through (‘en lidt barnlig skrift’ [a somewhat childish hand]), and in another hand ‘Sum Paul…’, the rest lost on the torn-off part; on its back, under a struck line where ‘Brade’ can be glimpsed, the price ‘… 4 taliro & 4 Gross.’; short astronomical notes in Tycho's hand inside the binding and on the back of the leaf, and one in the margin of the preface's tenth (unpaginated) page; Latin plant names at the head of 36 unpaginated pages in a hand other than Tycho's, plant remains in the gutter of 26 of them (microscoped by Jette Baagøe, re-housed in annotated envelopes of acid-free silk paper), further remains between pages without names, one plant name unattested before c. 1623 (Peter Wagner and Baagøe).

Examined and photographed at the museum on 20 October 2026. Fly-title recto: ‘tico brade ven:’ in a thin early hand, struck through once in the same ink; beside it a name obliterated under heavy black strokes (Thykier: ‘Tyge Brahe’), ringed in pencil; to the right, in a firmer hand with a flourish, ‘Sum Pauli’ [I am Paul's], complete to its last letter, what followed lost with the torn-away part; above it a smudged short word in another ink, and at the head, right, a short word in a darker ink, both unread; at the head, left, pencil ‘Acc. 1986/116’. Fly-title verso: ‘Sum Thÿchonis Brade’ under a heavy bar, ‘Sum’ and ‘Brade’ legible beside it; below it the purchase note ‘Eptg thaliro & 4 Grossj’ [bought for a thaler and 4 groschen]; beneath that, intact, a two-branch memorandum in bracket form — ‘Decl: Merid: … obliqua {Adde / Subtr.} diff. asc. ab Ascensione Recta — Septen: … obliqua {Subtr. / Adde} diff. Asc. ab Asc. R.’, ‘obliqua’ struck and ‘Recta’ written above it — a rule for passing from right to oblique ascension by the sign of the declination. Front pastedown: an ink note in the same early hand (‘Ascens: decl: Merid: … ex Asc: Recta / Septm: Sub S … hoc modo’, a companion rule); a pencil collation in German (‘… 70 (von 1–74) … 31–34 … 1 Doppelbl. …’, the trace of the Munich trade); pencil ‘1986/116’. Spine: a paper slip in the second compartment with ‘20’ and a damaged third figure consistent with 4, the figures written by hand in ink; nothing else lettered, no label surviving at the tail. Rear flyleaf: a faint ‘N 36’ at the head and a small blind-stamped cartouche near the gutter, both unread. Rear pastedown: in ballpoint ‘Gave jan. 1957 fra civiling. Oskar Ekman’ [gift, January 1957, from the engineer Oskar Ekman], the stamp HANDELS- OG SØFARTSMUSEET PÅ KRONBORG HELSINGØR – DANMARK [the Trade and Maritime Museum at Kronborg, Helsingør, Denmark], and ‘30:57’ in blue. Box: brown cloth clamshell, its gilt spine label lettered in English — JOHANN DE MONTEREGIO TABULAE DIRECTIONUM 1550 BOUND WITH PETRUS APPIANUS COSMOGRAPHIA 1553. TYCHO BRAHE'S COPY WITH AUTOGRAPH OWNERSHIP NOTES, ETC. — with ‘30:57’ written on it.

Reader's marks in the Regiomontanus: a grey marginal note in an early hand on the Octavum problema page (‘pro g. m. / 60 / sec. / 15 / tert. / differentia’ — Thykier's note on the tenth page of the preface); tallies ij iij iiij beside ‘Ille autem numerus polaris’; a red-brown stepped line drawn through the Tabula declinationum; a large pen flourish at the head of the Tabula ascensionum rectarum. In the Apianus: ‘Octava sphaera’ written at the ring of the Schema praemissae divisionis, ‘Wittenberga’ underlined in the German place table; on fol. 53r ‘Elzingborgum 35 46. 57 0’ (a superscript c above the name) and ‘Lunda 36 30 57 23’, on fol. 53v ‘Vesbÿ Gotlandiæ 47 5. 59 50’ and the fourth place, its letters R-?-n-s-f-a-l-?-h-?, ‘46 35 60 15’, all four in one ink and ductus. The plant names stand at the head of the preface leaves (Pulmonaria, Ranunculus aquaticus, Hedera arborescens …) in a later hand, and the envelopes of plant remains lie at their openings; read letter by letter from the frames on 21 October 2026: the purchase note is ‘Eptꝰ thaliro & 4 Groſs.’ — emptus thalero et 4 grossis — the sign after ‘Ept’ being the -us abbreviation, not a g; the pastedown rule reads in full ‘Aſcens. decl {Merid. [Adde, blotted out by the writer] / Septen. Sub} ex Aſc. Recta / in deſcenſionibꝫ contrario modo’, the same rule as the verso's bracket memorandum, whose surviving case labels are ‘…rom:’ and ‘…nes’; above the rule a darker ‘Angl. 5.’, unexplained; the smudged word above ‘Sum Paüli’ reads ‘Brÿſſe’ or ‘Chrÿſſe’ and stands inside a wet-wiped erasure of an earlier inscription, the head-right word ‘to bra[h/n][e/o]’; the heavy black strokes through the recto name and through the verso's ‘Sum Thÿchonis Brade’ are one ink and one campaign, a later owner's cancellation of both Brahe names, while the thin stroke through ‘tico brade ven:’ is earlier and different; the herbarium hand also wrote ‘Lamiꝫ’, ‘Liſimachia flore luteo’ and ‘Hÿacinthꝰ’; on fol. 53 ‘Lůnda lůndt’ and ‘Veſbÿ Gotlandiæ’ carry the u-mark and the diaeresis, and the fourth place is the museum's ‘Rumefalche’ (the tall letter is an f with a cross-bar), identified on 21 October 2026 as Schöner's — Luculentissima quaedam terrae totius descriptio (Nuremberg 1515), fo. 34r, ‘Gotlandiae insu. civita.: Bespue 47.5.59.50. Rumefalche 46.35.60.15.’ — a Clavus-derived pseudo-toponym of Gotland from the Ulm Ptolemy of 1486, so that both added lines on fol. 53v are copied, name and figures, from Schöner or from his source; a pencil ‘choo’ on the rear pastedown

Chain of custody
  1. route unknown
    The route out of Tycho's hands is undocumented. The fly-title (Thykier's ‘forperm’) carries two struck ownership lines — ‘tico brade ven:’ on the recto and ‘Sum Thÿchonis Brade’ [I am Tycho Brahe's] on the verso, as the museum reads them (Thykier 2003 read the recto's as ‘Tyge Brahe’ and ‘tico brahe ven’) — which may mark a transfer in his lifetime or a later owner's cancellation. A third inscription in a firmer hand, ‘Sum Pauli’ [I am Paul's], is the only name of a later owner the volume yields: Thykier read it as ‘Sum Paul…’ with the rest lost on the torn-off part of the leaf, but the examination of 20 October 2026 shows it complete to its last letter, so the owner's first name is all the leaf ever carried of him. The book was used as a herbarium by a hand other than Tycho's, one of whose plant names is unattested before c. 1623 (Thykier 2003, after Wagner and Baagøe), so it was in a plant-collector's hands after Tycho's death. It stands in no Prague catalogue and its first title carries none of the Jesuit, Chomutov or Crusius marks of the Bohemian roads. The spine label, a paper slip with the figures ‘20[4]’ written in ink, records a library between Tycho and the Munich trade that no source names (Thykier: ‘formodentlig katalognummeret i Tychos eller et senere bibliotek’ [presumably the catalogue number in Tycho's or a later library]; the dossier of 20 October 2026); the rear flyleaf adds a faint ‘N 36’ at the head and a small blind-stamped cartouche near its gutter — a second earlier number and a dealer's or restorer's mark, both unread (examined 20 October 2026). — Thykier 2003; Museernes Samlinger (M/S Museet for Søfart), the object record 1957:0030; Helsingør examination 2026
  2. Antiquariat Weiss & Co., Munich by 1932
    Prandtl 1932, the captions of his plates (pp. 2–3: ‘Seinerzeit im Besitze des Antiquariats Weiss & Co., München’ [at the time in the possession of the antiquarian firm Weiss & Co., Munich]) and p. 3 (‘kürzlich von München aus nach Amerika verkauft’ [recently sold from Munich to America] — wrong about the first step, see the next station); the Verzeichnis B entry for Band II names no location. The volume is in none of the firm's four digitised catalogues (1926–1930). — Prandtl 1932, pp. 292–293 (Sonderdruck pp. 2–3)
  3. Maggs Bros., London 1933–1938
    Catalogue 582 (1933), item 155, p. 92, £125, with every copy-specific identifier (the stamps, the half-torn fly-leaf, the purchase note, the Helsingborg note) and Prandtl cited for ‘our volume’; Catalogue 600 (1934), item 260, £125; Catalogue 666 (1938), item 451, p. 166, still £125, the entry reprinting Catalogue 600's text. The pencil ‘+125’ at the head of the front pastedown is this price. The firm's archive (British Library Add MS 89311) would name the seller. — Maggs 582 (1933), p. 92, item 155; Maggs 600 (1934), p. 208, item 260; Maggs 666 (1938), p. 166, item 451; New York Times Book Review, 28 May 1933, the issue (lending copy)
  4. Herbert Reichner, New York c. 1939–c. 1940
    Catalogue 1 ([1939]), item 72, probably $850 — Reichner was Prandtl's publisher in Vienna and emigrated to New York in 1938–39; the volume is in none of his Catalogues 2–9 (1940–48). — Reichner, Catalogue 1 ([1939]), the catalogue record (search-only; ‘Brade’ matches one page)
  5. route unknown from c. 1940
    The buyer from Reichner is not recorded; no American auction appearance 1930–58 (American Book Prices Current); Rare Book Hub has no record. Mortensen (1955, p. 25) knew of no such volume in Denmark, which bounds nothing: the volume was then in private hands, and the Tycho literature took no notice of the museum's report of 1957 until Norlind 1970. The one physical trace of the interval is the volume's box: a brown cloth clamshell box with a gilt spine label lettered in English (‘Tycho Brahe's copy with autograph ownership notes, etc.’), made in the English-speaking trade or for the American owner (examined 20 October 2026). — Mortensen 1955, p. 25; Helsingør examination 2026
  6. Oscar Ekman, engineer, and Lia Ekman, Copenhagen until 1957
    The museum's report of its acquisitions of 1956–57 (Klem, Årbog 1957, p. 8; Thykier 2003 dates the gift to January 1957 and names Oskar Ekman alone): ‘en gave fra fru Lia Ekman og afdøde ægtefælle, civilingeniør Oscar Ekman’ [a gift from Mrs Lia Ekman and her late husband, the engineer Oscar Ekman]. Oscar Ekman (b. Copenhagen 13 October 1885, owner of Nordisk Elektricitets Selskab from 1913, d. 16 September 1956) had been a life member of the museum's friends' society since 1944–45; Lia Ekman (née Pouplier, b. Hamburg 1894, d. 1965) remained one to her death. Their source for the volume is not recorded; at the sale of their library (Bruun Rasmussen 186, November 1965) it was shown as a withdrawn show-piece, as Erik Dal reported in Bogvennen 1966. The museum's accession note on the rear pastedown, ‘Gave jan. 1957 fra civiling. Oskar Ekman’ [gift, January 1957, from the engineer Oskar Ekman] (read 20 October 2026), enters the gift in January 1957 under Oscar's name, four months after his death: a gift in the dead man's name, made by his widow — which is what Klem's wording, Thykier's and Dal's ‘testamentarisk bortgivet’ [given away by bequest] each describe. The sale catalogue itself (Bruun Rasmussen 186, p. 111, lot 497, read 29 October 2026) lists the volume under ‘En Bog fra Tycho Brahe's Bibliotek’ for completeness only — ‘This copy will not be sold as it was given to the “Handels- og Søfartsmuseet” at Kronborg according to the last will of ingeniør Ekman’; the Danish text ‘foræret … af ingeniør Ekman og kan beses der i en af udstillingsmontrerne’ — so the gift was a bequest under Oscar's will, executed by his widow in January 1957, and the volume stood in a showcase at Kronborg in November 1965. — Handels- og Søfartsmuseet, Årbog 1957, p. 8; Kraks Blå Bog 1937, p. 276, s.v. Ekman; Bogvennen 1966, pp. 200–207, the passage on the Tycho volume; Thykier 2003; Helsingør examination 2026; Bruun Rasmussen 186 (1965)
  7. M/S Museet for Søfart (Handels- og Søfartsmuseet på Kronborg), Helsingør, inventory 1957:0030 from 1957
    The Ekmans' gift of 1956–57 (Årbog 1957; the museum's record); restored 1957 by Birgitte Dall. Deposited at the Royal Library's Håndskriftafdeling in 1986 as Acc. 1986/116 (the Kronik of 1987; the department's newsletter of November 2001), lent from there to the Ole Rømer Museum's exhibition ‘Tycho Brahes mange ansigter’ [Tycho Brahe's many faces], 25 October – 31 December 2001; in the museum's own keeping again, confirmed 13 October 2026, the date of return unrecorded. Examined and photographed at the museum on 20 October 2026: the Kronborg stamp and ‘30:57’ on the rear pastedown, ‘Acc. 1986/116’ in pencil on the fly-title and the pastedown and on the slip in the box, the plant envelopes in place, the box lettered in English. — Handels- og Søfartsmuseet, Årbog 1957, p. 8; Museernes Samlinger (M/S Museet for Søfart), the object record 1957:0030; Magasin 1987, pp. 31–33; Royal Library, Håndskriftafdelingen, Aktuelt (November 2001), ‘Udstillinger’, November 2001; Björklund 1992; Museet for Søfart 2026; Thykier 2003; Helsingør examination 2026

Notes

Prandtl’s Band II, the student binding of 1561: Regiomontanus’s Tabulae directionum (Tübingen, Ulrich Morhart, 1550) bound before Apianus’s Cosmographia in Gemma Frisius’s recension (Paris, Vivant Gaultherot, 1553; the colophon dated 1551). Prandtl recorded it in 1932 at Antiquariat Weiss & Co., Munich, and as sold to America; Gingerich (2002, p. 325) still placed it at an undisclosed location, calling it ‘the 1561 edition’ of the Apianus after the date on the binding. It had in fact returned to Denmark within a generation: the Handels- og Søfartsmuseet på Kronborg reported it among the acquisitions of 1956–57 as the gift of Lia Ekman and her late husband, the engineer Oscar Ekman, and Norlind placed it there in 1970 (pp. 338 and 361).

The museum’s record (Museernes Samlinger, inventory 1957:0030, priref 400004307, revised 16 June 2026) describes the volume leaf by leaf. The Regiomontanus: title, [43] pp. text, [268] pp. tables, a few notes in the head margin. The Apianus: title woodcut, fol. 1–74 with the volvelles, the cordiform world map inserted between fol. 34 and 35, the tables of positions from fol. 37; on fol. 53r–v Tycho has added four places with their figures, which the museum reads ‘Elzingborgum 35 46. 57 0 / Lunda Lundt 36 30 57 23 / Vesby Gotlandiæ 47 5. 59 50 / Rumefalche [?] 46 35 60 15’ — Helsingborg, Lund, Visby, and a fourth place the museum could not read. Quarto; stained, water-stained and wormed. Brown calf, blind-ruled mirror panels on both boards with originally gilt floral stamps, TVCHO BRADE stamped on the front board and ANNO 1561 on the rear, three raised bands, and on the spine the remnant of a label with the library shelfmark 204 — an owner between Tycho and the Munich trade whom no source names. The inscriptions: a note in ink on the front pastedown; on the fly-title, struck through, ‘tico brade ven:’, then ‘Tycho Brade’ and further ownership inscriptions, the leaf half torn and repaired; on its verso ‘Sum Thÿchonis Brade’ [I am Tycho Brahe's], struck through, the purchase note ‘Eptg [emptus] thalero & 4 Groshj’ [bought for a thaler and 4 groschen], and beneath it an astronomical note, partly lost. Prandtl’s ‘Vorsatzblatt’ [endpaper] with the note and the name three times is thus placed: the purchase note is on the verso of the fly-title, not on the pastedown. Kept in a cloth-covered box; restored in 1957 by Birgitte Dall, Copenhagen. The record’s two photographs show the front board, and the volume open in its box at the Regiomontanus title with the fly-title raised, its verso with the purchase note and the astronomical note towards the camera. The record is filed in the museum’s case ‘Deponeringer div:’ (deposits), which agrees with the Royal Library deposit of 1986.

The Danish record of the volume, in order. Klem’s museum report for 1956–57 (Årbog 1957, p. 8) lists it among the year’s acquisitions as ‘en gave fra fru Lia Ekman og afdøde ægtefælle, civilingeniør Oscar Ekman’ [a gift from Mrs Lia Ekman and her late husband, the engineer Oscar Ekman], bought by Tycho ‘i 1561’ for ‘1 Thaler, 4 Groschen’. Erik Dal in Bogvennen (1966, p. 205), reviewing the Ekman sale (Bruun Rasmussen auction 186, 1–4 November 1965), names among the show-pieces withdrawn as bequests ‘en bog fra Tycho Brahes bibliotek (til Kronborg — Rundetaarn bød samtidig meget højt, men forgæves på en lignende hos Sotheby)’ [a book from Tycho Brahe's library (to Kronborg — Rundetaarn at the same time bid very high, but in vain, on a similar one at Sotheby's)]: the volume was still the museum’s in 1965. The Kronik of Magasin fra Det Kongelige Bibliotek (2:1, 1987, p. 31) records its deposit in the Royal Library’s Håndskriftafdeling, the contact arranged by museumsleder Claus Thykier of Ole Rømers Museum, the museum on Kronborg remaining the owner — an earlier witness to the deposit than Björklund 1992. The Kronik prints the front-board stamp as ‘Tycho Brahe’, a normalisation of the museum’s reading TVCHO BRADE; it reports ‘adskillige notater med flere hænder, deriblandt Tycho Brahes’ [several notes in more than one hand, among them Tycho Brahe's], and gives the herbarium use as a suggestion that had not been examined (‘foreslået … endnu ikke kunnet undersøges’ [suggested … not yet possible to examine], p. 33), which Björklund 1992 then states as fact. The Royal Library’s newsletter of November 2001 shows the volume still on deposit (Acc. 1986/116), lent to the Ole Rømer Museum’s exhibition of that autumn, and the Nationalmuseet catalogue of 2006 reproduced it from a Royal Library photograph. The deposit has ended: the museum’s curator, Thorbjørn Thaarup, confirmed in writing on 13 October 2026 that the volume is part of the museum’s collection and is kept at the museum (‘en del af vores samling og opbevares hos os’ [part of our collection and kept with us]); when it returned from the Royal Library is not stated.

The twentieth-century route, established on 20 October 2026: Prandtl’s ‘sold from Munich to America’ was wrong about the first step. Maggs Bros. of London offered the volume in Catalogue 582 (1933), item 155, at £125 — the entry collates both works, gives the stamps, transcribes ‘Sum Tychonis Brade / Epts thaliro & 4 Gross’ [I am Tycho Brahe's / bought for a thaler and 4 groschen], notes the half-torn fly-leaf and the Helsingborg note, and cites Prandtl for ‘our volume’ — again in Catalogue 600 (1934), item 260, and still in Catalogue 666 (1938), item 451, p. 166, always at £125, the figure pencilled on the front pastedown; the New York Times reviewed the 1933 catalogue on 28 May 1933. It reached New York only with Herbert Reichner’s first catalogue ([1939], item 72, probably $850) and is in none of his later ones. Between about 1940 and 1956 nothing is known.

Three published readings do not hold. The Kronik’s argument of 1987 that the price in thaler and groschen is Saxon coin and that the Leipzig matriculation register enters Tycho under 16 October 1561 fails on both points: the Copenhagen Ptolemy of 1560 is itself priced in Joachimsthaler, and 16 October is the winter-semester heading, the rector entering the term’s students at its end (Norlind 1970, p. 16); the binding’s ‘Anno 1561’ and every other witness put the purchase in Copenhagen. The Helsingborg and Lund figures on fol. 53r are the book’s own printed values from the Scania table on fol. 41v, transcribed beside the Zealand–Scania island entry; Visby and the fourth place stand beside the printed Gotland entry on fol. 53v. Christianson 1967 (Isis 58, p. 201), citing Prandtl, puts the ‘Anno 1561’ stamp on the spine — a slip; it is on the rear board. The provenance is set out in the deep-research dossier of 20 October 2026.

Claus Thykier’s report of 13 January 2003 (Ole Rømer Museet; absorbed 20 October 2026) is the examiner’s own account: he inspected the volume at the museum’s library in 1985, arranged its deposit at the Royal Library in 1986 (acc. 1986/116), and records the gift as January 1957 from civilingeniør Oskar Ekman with further provenance unknown. He reads the front of the fly-title (his ‘forperm’) as ‘Tyge Brahe’ struck through and ‘tico brahe ven’ struck through, and in another hand ‘Sum Paul…’, the rest lost on the torn-off part; the price, under a struck line where ‘Brade’ can be glimpsed, as ‘… 4 taliro & 4 Gross.’ in Saxon coin, which led him to leave open a journey abroad in 1561 or a book sent by courier (both answered by the semester convention: Norlind 1970, p. 16). He describes the herbarium: plant names at the head of 36 unpaginated pages in a hand not Tycho’s, remains in the gutter of 26 of them identified under the microscope by Jette Baagøe and re-housed in envelopes with the modern names, one name unattested before c. 1623 (Peter Wagner), so that the user ‘formentlig ikke har haft kontakt med Tycho’ [probably had no contact with Tycho]; and an astronomical note in Tycho’s hand in the margin of the preface’s tenth page. The book is to be kept horizontal in its capsule so that the plant remains do not fall out.

Examined and photographed at the museum on 20 October 2026, the volume, its box and the examiner’s report of 2003 brought from the store. What the leaves settle: the fly-title recto carries ‘tico brade ven:’ in a thin early hand, struck through once in the same ink; a name obliterated under heavy black strokes, which Thykier read as ‘Tyge Brahe’; and, in a firmer hand with a flourish, ‘Sum Pauli’ [I am Paul's] — complete to its final letter, so the owner’s first name is all the leaf ever carried of him, and what stood on the torn-away part was not the rest of it; two further short words, one smudged, one at the head right, stay unread. The verso’s struck line is the museum’s ‘Sum Thÿchonis Brade’ — ‘Sum’ and ‘Brade’ are legible beside the bar — so the museum’s transcription and Thykier’s ‘a struck line where Brade can be glimpsed’ describe one line, and the recto’s ‘Sum Pauli’ is one of the museum’s ‘further ownership inscriptions’: the three witnesses agree once the two sides of the leaf are told apart. Beneath the purchase note the astronomical memorandum is intact — a two-branch rule in bracket form for passing from right to oblique ascension by the sign of the declination, ‘obliqua’ struck and ‘Recta’ written above it — and the ink note on the front pastedown is a companion rule in the same early hand. The front pastedown also carries a pencil collation in German, the trace of the Munich trade, and the Royal Library’s accession number in pencil, which stands again as ‘Acc. 1986/116’ at the head of the fly-title and on the slip kept in the box.

The spine label is a paper slip with the figures written in ink, ‘20’ and a damaged third figure consistent with 4; nothing else is lettered on the spine, and the larger label or patch that Prandtl’s plate of 1932 showed at the tail is gone. The rear flyleaf carries a faint ‘N 36’ at the head and a small blind-stamped cartouche near the gutter — a second earlier number and a dealer’s or restorer’s mark, both unread. The box is a brown cloth clamshell box with a gilt spine label lettered in English, ‘Tycho Brahe’s copy with autograph ownership notes, etc.’ — made in the English-speaking trade or for the American owner between 1933 and 1956, the one physical trace of that interval. The rear pastedown has the museum’s accession note in ballpoint, ‘Gave jan. 1957 fra civiling. Oskar Ekman’ [gift, January 1957, from the engineer Oskar Ekman], with the Kronborg stamp and ‘30:57’, the inventory number in its old form: the gift was entered in January 1957 under Oscar Ekman’s name, four months after his death on 16 September 1956 — which reconciles Klem’s ‘fru Lia Ekman og afdøde ægtefælle’ [Mrs Lia Ekman and her late husband], Thykier’s ‘January 1957 from civilingeniør Oskar Ekman’ and Dal’s ‘testamentarisk bortgivet’ [given away by bequest]: a gift in the dead man’s name, made by his widow.

The herbarium is confirmed: the plant names stand at the head of the preface leaves (Pulmonaria, Ranunculus aquaticus, Hedera arborescens …) in a later hand, and the envelopes of plant remains lie at their openings, so the box must stay flat. The four places on fol. 53r–v are in one ink and ductus, written in one campaign: ‘Elzingborgum’ with a superscript c, ‘Lunda’, ‘Vesbÿ Gotlandiæ’, and the fourth, whose letters read R-?-n-s-f-a-l-?-h-? and whose figures, set against the printed values, put it about half a degree west of Visby and a little north — the Swedish coast opposite Gotland, if the figures are to be trusted; the name, not the figures, must decide. The reader’s marks — tallies, a red stepped line through the declination table, a pen flourish, ‘Octava sphaera’, ‘Wittenberga’ underlined — are of several hands and dates, and none is attributed. Nothing here has been compared with a dated specimen of Tycho’s hand: ‘Tycho’s hand’ remains the attribution of Prandtl, the museum and Thykier. Read letter by letter from the frames on 21 October 2026. The purchase note is ‘emptus thalero et 4 grossis’ — the sign that the museum, Thykier and the examination of 20 October took for a g is the 9-shaped -us abbreviation. The ink rule on the front pastedown reads in full: oblique ascension, for a southern declination add, for a northern subtract, the ascensional difference from the right ascension; in descensions the contrary way — the writer blotted out his first ‘Adde’ and wrote the rule again on the fly-title verso as a bracket table, there striking ‘obliqua’ and writing ‘Recta’ above it. The smudged word above ‘Sum Pauli’ stands inside a wet-wiped erasure of an earlier inscription and reads ‘Brÿſſe’ or ‘Chrÿſſe’; the heavy black strokes through the recto name and through the verso's ‘Sum Thÿchonis Brade’ are one ink and one campaign — a later owner cancelled both Brahe names, and the thin stroke through ‘tico brade ven:’ is an earlier, different cancellation. Three more plant names were read (Lamium, Lysimachia flore luteo, Hyacinthus), the Octavum problema column in full, and the fourth place on fol. 53v confirmed as the museum's ‘Rumefalche’ (the tall letter has a cross-bar: f, not a long ſ; a first reading of 21 October took it for ſ and is withdrawn). The same day the name was identified: ‘Bespue 47.5.59.50.’ and ‘Rumefalche 46.35.60.15.’ are the two ‘cities’ of Gotland on fo. 34r of Johannes Schöner's Luculentissima quaedam terrae totius descriptio (Nuremberg 1515), the text to his globe, and Schöner's northern towns come from the Ulm Ptolemy of 1486, whose Scandinavian names are Claudius Clavus's (Bjørnbo and Petersen 1904, pp. 115 and 118) — Rumefalche is one of Clavus's invented Gotland names, not a place, and its figures are a position on a map, not an observation. Helsingborg and Lund carry Schöner's figures too (Elebogum 35.46.57.0, Lunda 36.30.57.23), which Apian's printed Scania table repeats; so all four lines on fol. 53 are Schöner's values, copied by the student from Schöner, or from Apian and Schöner together. The Visby name form points to the exemplar: the 1515 printing reads ‘Bespue’ alone, the later Nuremberg reprint Bjørnbo used (undated, 1518) reads ‘Bespue uel wisbuy’, and the writer put the real name ‘Vesbÿ’ — so either the reprint or the Ulm Ptolemy itself stood on his desk at Leipzig; which, is open. The hands are labelled descriptively: A the thin early hand of ‘tico brade ven:’, B the grey-brown hand of ‘Sum Thÿchonis Brade’ and the purchase note, C the firm dark hand of the pastedown rule, the verso memorandum and the fol. 53 places, D ‘Sum Pauli’, E the herbarium hand, F the German pencil collation. Hand C was compared feature by feature with the dated specimens of the Graphonomicon of Tycho Brahe — the horoscope prose of 1583, the observation narrative of 1597, the album leaf of 1599 — and with the unattributed index of 1563–65 in the Uppsala volume of 1563: ten concordances with the atlas (the looped uncial d, the u-mark, the diaeresis on y, the single-storey a, the long-ſ/round-s distribution, the ſc connection, the looped e, the capital A, a slant of 15–24 degrees inside the atlas band) and no discordance, but none of high distinctiveness, and the display forms that would decide do not occur in a working memorandum; against the Uppsala index the concordances are preponderant, but that hand is itself unattributed between Tycho at Leipzig and Vedel. The verdicts are inconclusive on both pairings. No hand is attributed: ‘Tycho's hand’ remains the attribution of Prandtl, the museum and Thykier, now recorded as contradicted by no feature and supported in kind by every one; a secure Vedel autograph of the 1560s–80s is what would decide it. Christianson (1967, p. 201) counts it with the pigskin volume of 1560 ([[vol:s-kl-47|18 J 318]]) as ‘two of his own “textbooks”’ still preserved; it is the earliest book to carry a purchase note beside an ownership entry in Tycho’s name — the hands of both unattributed, the comparison of 21 October 2026 inconclusive. The volume is not digitised; the museum’s record carries its two photographs (the front board; the volume open at the Regiomontanus title), and the examination of 20 October 2026 photographed the boards, the edges and the inscribed leaves. || 29 October 2026 — the six student volumes judged in the lecture session (the literature pack of this volume: 175 passages, 84 documents): this is the one surviving volume documented as Tycho’s before he left Denmark on 14 February 1562, by the boards alone. The place of purchase is an inference from the year, not a document; the groschen of the price are German money of account, unexplained for Copenhagen by any source, the house’s Joachimsthaler argument answering only the thaler; the tools of the binding have not been set against the Uppsala binding of 1563; ‘Sum Pauli’ is an owner no source dates. The volume’s return to Denmark — the Ekmans’ gift, entered in January 1957 by Lia Ekman in her late husband’s name — makes the one Copenhagen student volume a Danish holding, at Helsingør. || 29 October 2026 — the Ekman sale catalogue read in a copy in a private collection (Bruun Rasmussen 186, p. 111, lot 497): the volume is entered under ‘En Bog fra Tycho Brahe's Bibliotek’ for completeness only, not for sale, ‘given to the Handels- og Søfartsmuseet at Kronborg according to the last will of ingeniør Ekman’, and on view there in a showcase in 1965. The catalogue repeats the trade's description (‘en af de første, Tycho Brahe har ejet … han har skrevet sit navn deri samt et par notater og den pris’); what it adds is custodial — the bequest — not palaeographic.
