The 72 physical volumes identified as Tycho’s own (three of them under an identification now disputed), holding 131 of the catalogued works, by holding institution: the National Library of the Czech Republic first, the other holders after it; the table at the head sorts them by shelfmark, holder, binding, grade, road and first custodian, and each volume has a page of its own. Each record gives the shelfmark as the holder catalogues it, the earlier catalogue numbers, the marks that secure the identification and their grade, the contents, the holder’s own provenance note where one is published, the chain of custody, and the catalogue record and the facsimile where the holder publishes them. The one volume marked Tychoniana but not his and the three copies examined and ruled out follow in sections of their own.

For the Prague volumes the National Library’s own catalogue record, as published in Manuscriptorium, is quoted beneath the constituents: the binding and physical description, the former owners with the dates the catalogue attaches, provenance notes not already given, the library’s notes on each tract, the literature the record cites, and the digital copy where one exists — in the library’s Czech, verbatim. Where the entry of the printed manuscript catalogue has been converted into a database record, it is quoted too.

A key to the terms that recur in the library’s Czech notes: titulní list, titulní strana = title page; přídeští = pastedown; předsádka = flyleaf; rukou, rukopisný, rkp. = in manuscript; přípisek = inscription; razítko = stamp; exlibris; iniciály = initials; starší signatura = earlier shelfmark; supralibros; tužkou = in pencil; modře = in blue; pochází z knihovny = comes from the library of; jezuitská kolej = Jesuit college. In the catalogue’s physical lines s. = pages, l. = leaves, nečíslovaných = unnumbered.

Handwriting in the digitised volumes

An automated survey of the facsimile images of 29 digitised volumes locates handwriting and classifies it by kind (marginal notes, underlining, corrections, ownership inscriptions, stamps): 13,510 images examined, 1,023 of them carrying handwriting of some kind. It is not a leaf-by-leaf examination except where a record says so, and it attributes no hand. The Prague volumes carry, besides whatever Tycho wrote, the hands of earlier owners and the accession notes of the Jesuit houses, 1636–1675 (the Clementinum, Chomutov, Krumlov). Only the most informative images of each volume are listed, by image number, so that the facsimile can be opened at them; no reading of the annotations is offered here. The Uppsala volume’s facsimile is not online.
